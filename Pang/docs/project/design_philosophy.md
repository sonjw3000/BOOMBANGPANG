# Design Philosophy

## Bottlenecks are the Gameplay

The core challenge of the game is identifying, understanding, and resolving bottlenecks within the logistics network.

Congestion, overloaded workers, poor layouts, and unstable throughput are intended parts of gameplay rather than temporary problems to eliminate completely.

The player should constantly analyze and reorganize the logistics flow to maintain operational stability.

---

## Readability over Realism

Gameplay readability is prioritized over strict realism.

The player should quickly understand:
- what is happening
- why it is happening
- where the problem exists

Systems should provide visible and understandable feedback rather than relying on hidden calculations or excessive simulation depth.

---

## Information over Automation

The game should provide:
- operational data
- throughput information
- worker states
- bottleneck visibility
- logistics flow visualization

The player is expected to make strategic decisions manually.

Automation should support the player, not replace decision making entirely.

---

## Problems Create Gameplay

Gameplay emerges from operational instability.

Examples:
- worker congestion
- overloaded storage
- delayed logistics
- poor routing
- accidents
- infrastructure failures
- hazardous space environments

The goal is not to create a perfectly stable system immediately, but to continuously respond to growing operational pressure.

---

## Space Should Affect Gameplay

The sci-fi setting is not only visual flavor.

Space environments should directly influence gameplay, logistics, and incidents.

Examples:
- dangerous spacecraft landings
- low-gravity accidents
- vacuum-related explosions
- debris scattering across facilities
- infrastructure damage affecting logistics flow

The game should feel like an industrial logistics operation adapted to hostile off-world environments.

---

## Human Labor vs Automation

Humans and robots serve different strategic purposes.

Humans are:
- flexible
- adaptable
- general-purpose

But introduce:
- fatigue
- mistakes
- instability

Robots are:
- specialized
- stable
- scalable

But require:
- infrastructure
- investment
- maintenance

The player should balance flexibility, stability, cost, and scalability.

---

## Operational Pressure

The player should constantly experience pressure from:
- increasing order volume
- congestion
- limited infrastructure
- worker limitations
- delivery deadlines
- environmental hazards

The game is designed around the feeling of stabilizing a growing and increasingly chaotic logistics system.

---

## Incremental Complexity

Complexity should emerge gradually through system interaction.

Avoid introducing unnecessary mechanics that do not meaningfully contribute to:
- logistics flow
- bottlenecks
- throughput
- operational decision making

New systems should reinforce the core logistics gameplay rather than distract from it.
